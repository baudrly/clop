    Windows are ranked by the cosine between their DNA embedding and the embedding of your words: no keyword ever touches a sequence. The histogram shows all similarities; the highlighted bins are the windows lit up in the map.

    Write your own classifier: one text prompt per line. CLOP calls each window by its most similar prompt. Prompts can name a species, a biotype, both, or neither.

    CLOP trains a DNA encoder and a text encoder so that a sequence lands next to the words that describe it, as CLIP does for images and captions. Any caption then becomes a classifier: “mus musculus long non coding rna” is a prompt, and the call is the most similar prompt.

    The DNA encoder is a strand-invariant dilated CNN: a sequence and its reverse complement get the same embedding. Its pooling is length-invariant (mean and mean square over interior positions), and sequences longer than the training window are read as the mean of overlapping windows. The map shows held-out windows laid out in 3D with UMAP.
